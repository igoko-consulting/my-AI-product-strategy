# Proxy Interviews — Five Sessions Against the Prototype

**Method: proxy interviews.** Family members role-played each target persona and were walked through the live prototype. This was the best available within the time box, and it is recorded as what it is.

**What this evidences.** Comprehension. Five real people looked at real screens, so findings about whether the disruption-risk label reads without explanation, whether the confidence percentage is understood, and whether the three autonomy settings are distinguishable all hold — comprehension does not depend on the participant having been disrupted.

**What this does not evidence.** Willingness to pay, switching intent, or the cost and time figures quoted. Those come from role-played stories told by people who were not there. **Bet Validation therefore stays at 2/5** in the M6 evaluation, and the M1 reflection still records that no actual disrupted traveller and no carrier has been asked anything.

**Participants:** solo budget (UK) · mid-range couple, long-haul (EU) · family with children, long-haul package (US) · premium multi-leg (UK) · mid-range couple, multi-leg (US).

## Participant 1 — Ellie, 27, Bristol — Solo traveller, budget-conscious, short-haul city break — UK

*Books frequent long-weekend city breaks solo on budget carriers; price-led.*

### Screener

**Interviewer:** In the last two years, have you taken a short city-break trip where a flight or hotel booking was disrupted?

**Ellie:** Yes — Krakow, back in the spring. The hotel had overbooked when I got there.

**Interviewer:** Did you book and travel that trip on your own?

**Ellie:** On my own, yeah, I go on these trips solo most of the time.

**Interviewer:** When you pick a flight or hotel, is price normally the deciding factor?

**Ellie:** Pretty much always. Cheapest that's not a total dive, that's the one.

**Interviewer:** Roughly what did that trip cost you all in?

**Ellie:** About £180 for flights and three nights.

**Interviewer:** Are you free to talk me through it for about 20 minutes?

**Ellie:** Yeah, sure.

### Part 1 — The story

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time a city break went wrong. Take me through it in order.

**Ellie:** I landed in Krakow late evening, got to the hotel around 10pm, and they told me at check-in they'd overbooked — no room for me. The booking site I'd used just showed it as confirmed, no warning at all.

**Interviewer:** How did you find out?

**Ellie:** Standing at the desk. The receptionist just said it straight, "we don't have your room," which was not what I wanted to hear after a long day of travel.

**Interviewer:** Walk me through the first thirty minutes after you realised.

**Ellie:** I asked what they were doing about it, and they said they'd try to find somewhere nearby but couldn't promise anything. I ended up pulling out my phone and searching Booking.com myself for anything with availability that night, in a foreign city, tired, at 10:30pm.

**Interviewer:** What did it cost you?

**Ellie:** Found somewhere about twenty minutes away for the night, cost me an extra £70 on top of what I'd already paid for the original room. Original hotel said they'd refund that first night eventually — took about three weeks to actually land back in my account. So call it £70 out of pocket plus a good hour and a half of stress that evening.

**Interviewer:** Did you use any app, website or AI tool to help sort it out?

**Ellie:** Just Booking.com on my phone, filtering for anything with a room free that night. Nothing clever — just me, tired, scrolling.

**Interviewer:** Looking back, what did you wish existed at that moment?

**Ellie:** Something that had already spotted the overbooking before I even landed, and had a backup sorted, instead of me standing at a desk figuring it out myself.

### Part 2 — Behaviour since

**Interviewer:** Has that changed anything about how you book now?

**Ellie:** I read the cancellation policy properly now, and I screenshot my booking confirmation before I travel — feels a bit paranoid but there you go.

**Interviewer:** Did anyone sort any of it out for you?

**Ellie:** No, that was entirely me on the night. The original hotel refunded the one night eventually, that's it.

### Part 3 — The prototype

*[Ellie scrolls the search and results screens unprompted for a minute.]*

**Interviewer:** What do you make of that coloured label next to each trip?

**Ellie:** I'm guessing some kind of risk rating — how likely the trip is to go wrong.

**Interviewer:** And that percentage next to it?

**Ellie:** Match to what I said I wanted, maybe? Not sure at first glance, to be honest.

**Interviewer:** Now you know what the label means, would you have picked a different one of these trips, or the same one anyway?

**Ellie:** If two were close on price, yeah, I'd go lower risk every time after Krakow. If the riskier one were noticeably cheaper though, I'd probably still take my chances — that's just how I book.

*[Shown the guardian screen with the three authority settings.]*

**Interviewer:** Which of these would you pick, and why?

**Ellie:** "Ask first." I want to be told and to say yes, but I don't want to be the one hunting for the backup option myself again.

**Interviewer:** What would have to be true for you to pick the one above it — auto-rebook?

**Ellie:** Genuinely not sure I'd ever want it fully automatic for something like a hotel — I'd always want to at least see it first, even if it's the exact same price.

**Interviewer:** This holds the alternative for 45 minutes — is that enough time for you?

**Ellie:** Yeah, I'm glued to my phone most of the time, that's plenty.

*[Shown the disruption screen with the recommended fix and its confidence percentage.]*

**Interviewer:** What does that number tell you?

**Ellie:** How sure it is this is the right call. 94 sounds pretty solid.

**Interviewer:** Does it make you more or less comfortable approving it?

**Ellie:** More, yeah. Though I'd still want to know roughly what the gap is — is that 6% "might cost more" or "might not work at all"?

**Interviewer:** If you put your phone down for two hours, what do you expect happens next?

**Ellie:** Probably the held option disappears and I'm back to sorting it myself — which, after Krakow, is exactly what I'd expect.

### Part 4 — Value

**Interviewer:** At that moment during your own disrupted trip, what would you have paid to have it sorted?

**Ellie:** Maybe £40 or £50, there and then, just to not be the one doing it. Less than what I ended up losing anyway.

**Interviewer:** Before the trip, would you have paid anything for someone to watch it for you?

**Ellie:** Only if it were tiny — a couple of quid, less than a coffee. Any more and I'd just risk it, same as always.

**Interviewer:** Would you have booked somewhere else to get this?

**Ellie:** If it were free or basically free and bundled in, sure. I wouldn't pay more or go out of my way for it though.

**Interviewer:** If this came free because you were a loyalty member, versus a flat fee every time, versus only paying if something went wrong — which would you rather?

**Ellie:** Pay-only-if-it-goes-wrong, easily. No status with anyone so loyalty-free's not really on the table, and a flat fee for something that might never happen feels like a waste.

**Interviewer:** You just said you'd happily have paid £40–£50 in the moment without blinking — why does a small amount before feel different to that?

**Ellie:** Because in the moment it's a guaranteed problem I know is real. Before travelling, it's a maybe — I'm paying for something that might just never happen to me on that trip. I know that's not how insurance works, but that's honestly how it feels.

**Interviewer:** The standalone version is $6.49 at booking plus $35 if it resolves a disruption. What's your first reaction?

**Ellie:** The $35 stings a bit when you're already having a bad day, but it's nothing next to the £70 I actually lost in Krakow, so — fine, honestly. I'd still rather it were just free and baked into a site I already trust though.

## Participant 2 — Anke & Lukas, early 30s, Berlin — Couple, mid-range spend, long-haul beach or resort trip — EU

*Booked a two-week trip to Mauritius, connecting through Dubai.*

### Screener

**Interviewer:** In the last two years, have you taken a long-haul beach or resort holiday where a booking was disrupted?

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: Yes — Mauritius, last autumn. We missed our connection in Dubai.

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: Just the two of us.

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: We were stuck together the whole night, yes.

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: Sure, go ahead.

### Part 1 — The story

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time a long-haul trip went wrong.

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: We flew Emirates, Berlin to Dubai then on to Mauritius. The Berlin leg was delayed on the tarmac for almost two hours before we even took off, no real explanation, and that ate our whole connection window in Dubai.

**Interviewer:** How did you find out, and who told you first?

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: The crew told us mid-flight that we'd likely miss the connection, but with no real detail about what happens next.

**Interviewer:** Walk me through the first thirty minutes.

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: We landed and ran, obviously, but the gate was already closed. Then it was the transfer desk, maybe forty minutes in a queue with everyone else off the same flight.

**Interviewer:** What did it cost you?

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: An overnight airport hotel in Dubai, about €130. And we lost the first night at the resort plus half the next day, prepaid all-inclusive, maybe €200 of value just gone, no refund from the resort.

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: The Emirates app a bit, and I called the resort directly once we got a number from our booking confirmation.

**Interviewer:** What did you wish existed at that moment?

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: Something that knew we'd miss it before we even landed in Dubai, and had already sorted a hotel and told the resort — instead of us working it out ourselves at a transfer desk at midnight.

### Part 2 — Behaviour since

**Interviewer:** Has it changed how you choose flights or connections now?

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: We build in much more buffer on connections now, and I actually read up on EU261 properly after that trip.

**Interviewer:** Did anyone sort it out for you?

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: Emirates eventually paid EU261 compensation, around €400 each, but that took about six weeks of emailing. Nobody helped us that night itself.

### Part 3 — The prototype

*[Both scroll the search and results screens.]*

**Interviewer:** What do you make of that coloured label next to each trip?

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: Disruption risk for that specific route — a tight connection versus a direct one, I'd guess.

**Interviewer:** And that percentage?

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: How well it matches what we told it we wanted.

**Interviewer:** Would the label have changed which of these you'd have picked?

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: Genuinely, yes. We've learned that the expensive way — we'd pay a bit more now for the option marked low risk over something a bit cheaper but medium or high.

*[Shown the guardian screen.]*

**Interviewer:** Which of these would you pick, and why?

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: Ask first, for both of us really. We want to see it before it's final, especially somewhere unfamiliar.

**Interviewer:** What would push you to the one above it — auto?

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: If the cap were high enough to actually cover a like-for-like hotel and transfer without us having to argue about cost, I could see it — but honestly, even then, I think we'd still want to see it first. It's a big trip for us.

*[Shown the disruption screen and confidence percentage.]*

**Interviewer:** What does that number tell you, and does it make you more or less comfortable?

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: More comfortable — we're used to airlines telling us things that turn out to be wrong, so a number from an independent tool feels more trustworthy, oddly.

**Interviewer:** The recommended fix also mentions no change fee under EU261 — does that mean anything to you, and does it change your trust?

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: Of course, we've used EU261. But it took six weeks to get paid. This is immediate, at the moment it actually matters. We'd still want this even knowing EU261 exists.

**Interviewer:** If you put your phone down for two hours, what do you expect happens next?

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: I'd assume the hold lapses, but I'd expect it to message us again rather than just quietly give up.

### Part 4 — Value

**Interviewer:** At that moment, what would you have paid to have it sorted?

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: Combined, maybe €150 to €200, just to be moved straight into a hotel and have the resort told, rather than us doing it ourselves at a transfer desk.

**Interviewer:** Before the trip, would you have paid anything for the watching?

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: Yes, something small — more willing than for a short trip, because long-haul the stakes are higher and the fixes are harder to do yourself.

**Interviewer:** Would you have booked somewhere else to get this?

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: If it clearly handled this exact situation better, yes, we're not that loyal to Emirates after this.

**Interviewer:** If this came free via loyalty, versus a flat fee, versus only paying if something went wrong — which would you rather?

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: We have some status, so free would be nice, but a few euros each before booking feels fine too — it's predictable, and we know what we're getting.

**Interviewer:** Reaction to the $6.49 + $35 split?

**Anke/Lukas:** Lukas: The upfront part is nothing. The resolution fee, on the day itself, feels a bit like being charged while you're already down — we'd rather it were capped lower right at the moment of crisis, or folded into the booking fee.

**Interviewer:** You told me EU261 compensation eventually arrived but wasn't enough in the moment — isn't $35 exactly the small price for solving the in-the-moment problem you said mattered most?

**Anke/Lukas:** Anke: [pause] That's... fair, actually. I think it's less about the number and more about the optics of a fee landing on the worst day of the trip. If it were framed differently — like, already included, and we just see a message saying "this was covered" — I think we'd feel completely different about the exact same €35.
